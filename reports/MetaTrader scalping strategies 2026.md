# Gold Breakout Survives Costs; "100%" Never Does

No scalping technique works 100 percent of the time, and every MetaTrader system we found displaying a near-100% win rate buys it with martingale, grid or no-stop-loss mechanics that eventually return the account in one move: the clearest 2026 example is an MQL5 signal with a 79.5% win rate and "709% growth" that the platform suspended with equity 97% below balance. Among techniques that place a hard stop on every trade, the single best-evidenced one as of 5 November 2026 is the gold (XAUUSD) Asian-range breakout at the London open: a 03:00–08:00 server-time range, OCO stop orders at 08:00, a stop at the far side of the range capped at 0.6% of price, a 2.5R target, everything flat by 21:00, one trade a day. Our own independent backtest of that exact rule on 35 months of minute data (January 2024 to 4 November 2026), charging 42 points of spread, commission and slippage per round trip, produced a **profit factor of 1.33 over 603 trades, +283 points ($2.83/oz) per trade with a 95% confidence interval entirely above zero, a 21.5% maximum drawdown at 1% risk (+112% on the account), and a result none of 50 coin-flip-direction runs on the same entries came close to** (random mean −41 points, best seed +128). The caveats are as large as the result: **65% of the profit came from 2026, 2024 and 2025 pooled are statistically indistinguishable from zero, the best 5% of trades supplied 118% of the net, and only 18 of 35 months were positive**. Everything else — M1/M5 indicator scalps, Asian-session "night scalping", EUR/USD and GBP/USD session breakouts, the academic EUR/USD time-of-day pattern, index opening-range breakouts on CFDs — is flat or negative after realistic costs in our tests and in every independent test we found. Verified long-lived scalping records are correspondingly rare: of roughly 4,275 ranked MQL5 signals, **15 (0.35%)** combine three-plus years of history, positive growth, a drawdown under 30% and sub-four-hour holds, and most of those now earn low single digits a year. An honest scalping system in 2026 looks like PF 1.2–1.5, a 40–50% win rate at a 2–2.5R target (or 65–75% at a sub-1R target), 15–25% drawdowns and mid-single-digit to low-teens annual returns at 1% risk; anything smoother is a sales page.

Throughout, "our backtest" means the two independent studies in this research folder: round one tested 46 common retail scalping variants on EURUSD and XAUUSD M1/M5 data, and round two tested the exact published gold London-range rules, an ATR-scaled variant, seven-cross night scalping and the Breedon–Ranaldo time-of-day pattern. Both ran on Forexite bid M1 data (1,047 daily archives, zero missing days, cross-validated against Dukascopy), with closed-bar signals, next-open fills, intrabar stop checks on M1 highs and lows, both-touched-in-one-bar counted as a loss, bootstrap confidence intervals and random-direction benchmarks; the code and result CSVs are in `backtest_code/` ([round one](../research_notes/MetaTrader%20scalping%20strategies%202026/independent_backtest_results.md); [round two](../research_notes/MetaTrader%20scalping%20strategies%202026/independent_backtest_round2.md)). Nothing in them uses vendor backtests, Myfxbook pages or marketing figures.

## Near-100% win rates are bought with ruin, not skill

The arithmetic is unforgiving and simple. A doubling martingale with a leg cap L wins 1 − (1/2)^L of its baskets and loses 2^L − 1 units when the basket fails, so "before costs, the net result is zero. After costs, it is negative"; the published backtest behind that statement ran four leg caps on four majors from December 2023 to September 2026 and found that **13 of 16 settings lost money while every one of them won 94–100% of baskets**, including a EUR/USD 10-leg version that won 99.8% of baskets, was up about $2,600 by early 2025, then gave up $2,916 on a single failed basket ([Forexop](https://forexop.com/martingale-trading-system-overview/)). With a six-leg cap and 50/50 trades, one failed cycle cancels 63 successful ones and the chance of at least one failure in 100 cycles is 79% ([PineConnector](https://www.pineconnector.com/blogs/pico-blog/martingale-grid-risk-of-ruin)). Capital does not buy safety: on a 20-pip EURUSD grid with a 2× multiplier, a $1,000 account dies at step 6, $10,000 at step 9 and $100,000 at step 12, so "increasing your capital by a factor of 10× only buys you roughly 3 additional grid steps", and a $10,000 gold grid is liquidated in five steps, about an $8 move ([FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/martingale-survival-test-measured-runs-account-failure)). The win rate therefore carries no information about expectancy; it is a property of the sizing rule.

The platform records show this playing out in real accounts in 2024–2026. The MQL5 signal "MagicGW audcad L" displayed 709% growth, 2,184 trades with 79.5% profitable and a profit factor of 2.24, alongside a balance of $279.63 against equity of $51.51, a relative drawdown of 77% by balance and 97% by equity, an annual forecast of −100% and the banner "Subscription not permitted... Current drawdown is dangerous for subscribers"; subscribers reported the provider doubling the account balance to mask drawdown before a review headed "BLEW ACCOUNT" in October 2026 ([MQL5 Signals 2280569](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/2280569)). Waka Waka, a grid still sold for $2,800 under the claim of "8+ years of live track record with +12,000% account growth" ([MQL5 Market 66317](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/66317)), has an official MQL5 signal at **−95.19% growth with a 91.8% drawdown, PF 1.03 and a 73.9% win rate** ([MQL5 Signals 995287](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/995287)), and one of its real Myfxbook accounts went from a $6,021 peak to $0.00 in three days of February 2022 ([Oreshnikbot](https://oreshnikbot.com/waka-waka-ea-myfxbook-analysis/)). Quantum Emperor, the best-selling EA on the MQL5 Market with roughly 5,000 purchases, "bumped into a 70% drawdown in June" 2024 ([MQL5 blog 758268](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/758268)); its current official signal shows a 90.76% win rate with an average loss 4.65 times the average win and a 40.5% maximal balance drawdown ([MQL5 Signals 2332481](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/2332481)). Its gold successor Quantum Queen X, launched in July 2026 and rated 4.08 from 87 reviews, collected a cluster of reviews in the first week of October 2026 reporting €700 to €0, $2,500 to $20 and $3,000 accounts at "low-to-medium risk" going to zero ([MQL5 Market 185687](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/185687)). A 13-year gold grid test by a vendor who sells the alternative puts the mechanism in one line: a tuned recovery grid won **98.5% of 844 trades and lost 23.2%**, with a largest loss of $4,425 against a second-worst of $132 ([MQL5 blog 776032](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776032)). A forum poster's client EA "needed 88.50% to break even and won 94.13%; 45 of its 801 deals accounted for all its earnings" ([MQL5 forum 517101](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/517101)). The practical screen traders have converged on is that a win rate above 90% combined with a largest loss at least ten times the average win is "delayed risk, not real edge" ([MQL5 forum 502291](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/502291/page7)).

The base rates make a 100% claim an inversion of the entire industry. ESMA's 2018 analysis found 74–89% of retail CFD accounts lose money ([ESMA via NBS](https://nbs.sk/en/news/esma-agrees-to-prohibit-binary-options-and-restrict-cfds-to-protect-retail-investors/)); the 14 UK brokers' rolling-twelve-month disclosures in 2026 average 70% losers, with the best (eToro) at 51% ([The Investors Centre](https://www.theinvestorscentre.co.uk/trading/statistics/cfd-trading/)); OANDA's US disclosures show 66–69% of accounts unprofitable each quarter through June 2026 ([OANDA](https://www.oanda.com/us-en/legal/risk-disclosure/)); ASIC's REP 828 found 68.42% of 195,386 Australian retail CFD clients realised a net loss in FY2024 ([compilation](https://enlightenedstocktrading.com/what-percentage-of-traders-lose-money/)). Those are quarterly snapshots; the panel studies that follow the same people are worse for frequent traders, which is what a scalper is. Of 19,646 Brazilians who began day trading, 3.0% of those who persisted more than 300 days were net positive and 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage ([Quantpedia summary of Chague et al.](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/)); in Taiwan "less than 1% of the day trader population is able to predictably and reliably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees" ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24)); across 300,000-plus prop-firm accounts, 7% of participants ever received a payout, averaging about 4% of the account size ([Finance Magnates](https://financemagnates.com/forex/prop-firm-e8-markets-warns-retail-traders-off-cfd-brokers-as-industry-leans-harder-on-educational-labels/)). The CFTC's own advisory lists "Claims of 100% 'win' rates" as a fraud warning sign ([CFTC Release 8854-24](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8854-24)), and an audit of a Telegram channel advertising "95%—even 100%—wins" on gold found 34% of its posted signals correct ([coinspot.io](https://coinspot.io/en/reviews/xauusd-scalper/)). Even the most flattering firm-published scalper profile illustrates the mechanism: FTMO's September 2026 case study of a gold scalper who made +25.1% in ten days on 572 trades at a 50% win rate highlights a trade with "no Stop Loss" and a worst day of −$9,937 against a $10,000 daily-loss limit, a margin of $62.93 ([FTMO](https://ftmo.com/en/blog/how-a-gold-scalper-secured-50333-in-2-weeks/)).

## Ranked by evidence, one family survives costs and ten do not

Evidence for scalping techniques comes in tiers that differ enormously in what they can tell you, and the ranking below puts independent, cost-inclusive tests over large samples at the top and vendor-produced tester screenshots at the bottom. Our two backtests are independent of every vendor and charge explicit costs; the MQL5 signal pages are broker-fed and real-badged but vendor-controlled and survivor-biased; the academic papers are rigorous but mostly pre-date 2020 and model costs as interdealer spreads; the vendor tests on MQL5 almost never state spread, commission or delay. Round one established the baseline that frames everything else. Across 46 variants of the templates retail content recommends — 9/21 and 5/13 EMA pullbacks, Bollinger(20,2) fades with and without RSI, RSI(2) extremes, Stochastic-plus-EMA50 crosses, a session VWAP fade, an Asian-range London breakout and four New York opening-range variants, on M1 and M5, for EURUSD and gold — **45 of 46 had negative expectancy after a base round-trip cost of 1.2 pips on EURUSD or 42 points on gold, and not one had a post-cost confidence interval above zero under any cost case** (our round one, `independent_backtest_results.md`). The EURUSD indicator scalps lost between −0.83 and −1.33 pips per trade against a 1.2-pip cost, with profit factors of 0.09–0.45 on M1 and 0.40–0.79 on M5; their gross edge before costs ranged from −0.13 to +0.37 pip, and a coin-flip baseline with the same ATR stop and target produced −1.22 to −1.33 pips net, indistinguishable from the indicators. The reason is mechanical: average M1 stops were 1.3–2.1 pips on EURUSD, so the base cost was 58–94% of the risk on every trade, and risking 1% of a $10,000 account on a 1.5-pip stop requires 5–8 lots, beyond a 30:1 leverage cap on 90–98% of M1 trades. Win rates that retail material quotes at 60–70% came out at 24–27% for EMA pullbacks on M1 once the stop was checked against intrabar highs and lows. Every indicator scalp lost in 2024, in 2025 and in 2026, and the first-half and second-half expectancies sat within 0.15 pip of each other, which is what a stable cost drag looks like.

Independent tests elsewhere say the same thing with different data. A standardised library of ten-year backtests (0.02% commission per side, no spread modelled) has the EUR/USD H1 London breakout at PF 0.87 and −28% over 1,230 trades, a GBP/USD M15 version at PF 0.72 with every calendar year negative, a DAX VWAP band fade at PF 0.64 and −98.6% despite a 59% win rate, MACD+RSI at PF 0.80, Bollinger+Stochastic at 0.77 and an ICT kill-zone FVG rule at 0.80; its only intraday winners were long-only gold trend rules on H1/H4 that still trailed buy-and-hold ([Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/london-breakout-eur-usd); [Backtrex GBP/USD](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/london-breakout-gbp-usd); [Backtrex VWAP](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/vwap-band-fade-dax); [Backtrex gold EMA](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/ema-8-21-crossover-gold)). A 2026 forum study applied a multiple-testing-corrected protocol to a discretionary-style scalper and found zero survivors among 31 features, all 48 target/stop combinations negative after cost, and a best real t-statistic of 3.65 against a median of 3.81 for the best finding on random data ([MQL5 forum 517101](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/517101)). For index opening-range breakouts, the two academic papers that report 33% and 41.6% annualised returns charge commissions only, assume fills at the printed price and never use the word "slippage" ([Zarattini & Aziz](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Can-Day-Trading-Really-Be-Profitable.pdf); [Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/A-Profitable-Day-Trading-Strategy-For-The-U.S.-Equity-Market.pdf)); the independent replication on five index CFDs from 2015 to 2026 reproduced the gross +0.131R on NQ and found **+0.002R net on NQ and −0.04 to −0.08R on SPX, Dow, DAX and FTSE** ([MQL5 blog 776235](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)). For ICT, the largest systematic test (16 years of NQ and ES minute data, 25,000 sampled configurations) found 1.7% survived a luck correction and cold run, the famous time windows were "no better than average", and only the liquidity-sweep requirement consistently added edge ([Gistly summary](https://gist.ly/youtube-summarizer/i-backtested-25000-ict-silver-bullet-trades)), while a frozen mechanical ICT ruleset on EURUSD M15 lost 15.1R over 125 trades at PF 0.81 before any spread was charged ([Offbeat Forex](https://offbeatforex.com/is-ict-strategy-profitable/)).

The academic literature on FX intraday patterns is consistent with these results and adds a decay story. Simple technical rules earned genuine excess returns in the 1970s and 1980s, but "these profit opportunities had disappeared by the early 1990s for filter and moving average rules" ([Neely, Weller & Ulrich](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2006/2006-046.pdf)). The best-identified short-horizon FX anomaly today, the V-shaped reversal around the Tokyo, ECB and London fixes, is 2–3 basis points, has shrunk from −3.49 bp (2010–13) to −0.63 bp (2014–19) for the dollar portfolio, and is "significantly negative" after costs for anyone paying the spread (−7.34 bp per day on AUD), with only liquidity providers earning it ([Krohn, Mueller & Whelan](https://www.wu.ac.at/fileadmin/wu/d/ri/isk/VSFX_2022/Paper/Session_III_1a_Krohn__foreign_Exchange_Fixings_Returns_Around_The_Clock_0712.pdf)). The older Breedon–Ranaldo rule — short EUR/USD 07:00–13:00 GMT, long 13:00–21:00 — made 6–7% a year at Sharpe 1.3 and 0.9 on 1997–2007 EBS data ([Breedon & Ranaldo](https://swlb1.aeaweb.org/conference/2009/retrieve.php?pdfid=301)). Our T4 ran that rule on 2024–2026 EURUSD minute data: **+0.21 and +0.01 pips per day gross on the two legs, +161 pips in total over 35 months, Sharpe 0.09, confidence interval [−1.45, +1.61], inside the random band, and −1.08 pips per leg after 1.2 pips of cost**; the year signs alternate (our round two, T4). The pattern is gone.

Vendor numbers sit at the bottom of the ranking because of how reliably they degrade when anyone else looks. The gold London-range result that round two set out to replicate was published on 13 October 2026 by an author who had listed the identical EA for sale three days earlier, states no spread, commission or delay, uses a single train/test split rather than a walk-forward, and posts its promised demo results only in a buyers-only comments tab ([MQL5 blog 777540](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540); [MQL5 Market 200590](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/200590)). A gold M1 scalper advertised at PF 4.19 in a real-tick backtest ([MQL5 blog 775822](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/775822)) runs at **PF 1.28 with a 29% drawdown and 17 pips of copy slippage on its own vendor's live signal**, where the average hold is 54 seconds ([MQL5 Signals 2390825](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/2390825)). An independent stress test of the best-selling gold breakout EA found that switching from M1 OHLC to real ticks cut its win rate from 78.3% to 71.4% ([MQL5 blog 776719](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776719)); a gold EA vendor admits the same strategy returned about 40% a year in the Strategy Tester and 12% live ([MQL5 blog 770439](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/770439)); and MetaQuotes' own demonstration shows one breakout EA swinging from +169 to −467 to −97 purely by changing the tick-modelling mode ([MQL5 Article 2612](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/2612)). When we tested the heavily marketed night-scalping and index-ORB rules ourselves, the vendor-shaped results evaporated; when we tested the gold London-range rule, they held, which is why it earns the next section.

## The gold London-range breakout is the one rule that survives, and 2026 does the heavy lifting

The exact rule we tested, taken from the published description, is this. Build the Asian range from the M1 highs and lows between 03:00 and 08:00 broker server time, where server time is New York time plus seven hours (UTC+2 in US winter, UTC+3 in US summer, the convention of IC Markets, Pepperstone and most MT5 brokers, so that the daily rollover falls at 00:00 server). Skip the day if the range is below 0.30% or above 1.60% of price. At 08:00 place a buy stop 20 points ($0.20) above the range high and a sell stop 20 points below the range low as a one-cancels-other pair; cancel whatever is unfilled at 21:00. The stop-loss sits at the far side of the range (plus buffer), but never more than 0.6% of the entry price away; if the range is wider than that, the stop moves inside it. Take profit at 2.5 times the stop distance. Move the stop to break-even when the trade reaches +1.5R. Close everything still open at 21:00 server. One fill per day, 1% of equity risked per trade. Our engine simulated every element, including the break-even logic and the same-bar tie-break, and an independent re-derivation against the raw minute arrays found zero violations across the 603 fills (our round two, section 4). The results are in the table; the units are gold points, where one point is $0.01 per ounce or $1 per standard lot.

Three things in that table are good news for the rule. First, the vendor's headline numbers replicate on data he never touched: our PF 1.33 against his 1.38, 17 trades a month against his 16, +112% against his +137% over a longer window, a 21.5% drawdown against his 17.3%, and a 2026 PF of 1.52 next to his 2026 hold-out PF of 1.61 ([MQL5 blog 777540](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)); whatever its weaknesses, the published backtest is not fabricated and is not an artefact of one broker's ticks. Second, the direction carries information. Coin-flip direction at identical entry bars, fill prices, stop distances, targets, break-even and time exits averaged −41 points after costs, and none of 50 seeds reached the rule's +283; none did in 2024 or 2026, and three of 50 did in 2025. Third, it is not simply long-gold beta. Longs (330 trades, +256 per trade) and shorts (273 trades, +317) contributed almost identical net points; the short side lost in 2024 and 2025 and made +940 per trade in 2026, the year gold fell 6% with a 28% drawdown; a plain long from 08:00 to 21:00 on the same eligible days lost 59 points a day; and trade results have zero correlation with the day's signed gold return and 0.47 correlation with its absolute size. Costs barely matter here, because the average stop is $18.91 per ounce and the base cost is 2.2% of it, so gross, spread-only, base and expensive cases differ by only 50 points per trade.

The bad news is in the same table and in the trade distribution. **2026 supplied 65% of the net points with 31% of the trades, and the 416 trades of 2024–2025 pooled give +144 points with a confidence interval of [−36, +349]**: a trader who started in January 2024 would have been unable, after two years, to tell this rule from noise. The median trade is −0.07R and the mean +0.16R; the ten best trades are 42% of the net and the best 30 (5%) are 118% of it, so removing the best 5% leaves −53 points per trade; only 18 of 35 months and 9 of 12 quarters were positive; the longest losing streak was 15 trades. The profit sits almost entirely in the 352 days whose Asian range exceeded 0.6% of price, where the cap pulled the stop inside the range (+499 per trade, CI [+164, +811]); the 251 narrower days gave −18 points (CI [−212, +177]). Those wide days are 2025 and 2026 days: gold's average 03:00–08:00 range went from $11.6 (0.41% of price) in 2024 to $26.1 (0.64%) in 2025 to $51.5 (0.93%) in 2026, matching the independent daily-range series from IC Markets data of $33.02, $60.70 and $120.50 for the full day ([MQL5 blog 778649](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/778649)). What this rule is, then, is an intraday trend-day capture whose payoff scales with volatility, in a gold market that went +14.6% in 2023, +25.5% in 2024 and a "multi-decade record" +67.4% in 2025 before a record $5,307 fix on 28 January 2026 and a 21–23% decline to $4,176 by 30 September ([WGC Dec 2025](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-market-commentary-december-2025); [WGC Jan 2026](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-market-commentary-january-2026); [WGC Sep 2026](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-market-commentary-september-2026)). Whether it works in a calm gold market, such as 2022 (+0.4%) or 2024's narrow Asian ranges, is exactly what the 2024 result says it cannot demonstrate.

The parameter surface is reassuring in sign and unimpressive in significance. All 36 cells of a take-profit × buffer × range-window grid were positive after base costs and all 36 stayed positive at the expensive cost, but only 23 had confidence intervals above zero, and none of the twelve cells using a 04:00–09:00 range did; shifting the range one hour later cuts expectancy to +171 with a CI that includes zero, so the result depends on where the box is drawn. The buffer is irrelevant (0, 20 and 50 points give +284, +283, +281). Break-even at 1.5R does not help: the same rule without it makes +295. A 2.5–3R target beats 1.5–2R. Letting unfilled orders expire at 12:00 instead of 21:00 improves the per-trade result to +320 on 526 trades and halves the drawdown to 16%. Removing the 0.30–1.60% range filter changes nothing per trade (+270, CI above zero) and adds 119 trades, so the filter is not the source of the edge. The one-sided versions are weaker than the two-sided rule: long-only +147 (not significant), short-only +227 (not significant), and filtering by the 50-day SMA direction helps the with-trend half (+377, CI above zero) but leaves the counter-trend half positive too (+206), with the filtered one-sided version at +284 on fewer trades, no better than the plain rule. Round one's own Asian-range breakout, run on different conventions (a 00:00–07:00 UTC range, a one-times-range target, a 12:00 UTC flat), made only +35 points per trade with a CI of [−118, +201]; the 2.5R target and the 21:00 close are load-bearing, and small changes in the published rules move the result from "noise" to "significant".

The runners-up are instructive mainly for how much weaker they are. The ATR-scaled variant in the free MQL5 CodeBase EA — range filter 0.15–0.70 of daily ATR(14), buffer 0.15 ATR, stop 1.2 ATR from entry regardless of range, target 1.8R, orders expiring at 12:00 ([MQL5 CodeBase 75586](https://www.mql5.com/en/code/75586)) — gave PF 1.52 and +470 points per trade on gold with a CI of [+33, +980], but 96% of its trades ended at the 21:00 flat because a $68 stop and a $122 target are rarely reached in a day; that is "enter on the breakout and hold to the close" in disguise, worth only 0.04R per trade and +10.6% at 1% risk over 35 months, with 77% of the net from 2026 and all of it from the sell side (buy-stop-only +57, not significant). On EURUSD the identical rule gave +0.0 pips after costs on 365 trades (our round two, T2), consistent with the published PF 0.87–1.03 attempts to port London breakouts to EURUSD and GBPUSD ([Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/london-breakout-eur-usd); [cTrader product 5168](https://ctrader.com/products/5168)). The night-scalping candidates failed for a reason worth understanding: pooled over seven crosses, a filtered Bollinger fade and two range-limit fades showed +0.28 to +1.04 pips per trade at base costs with CIs above zero, yet entries in the hour around the 00:00 server rollover — 19–30% of trades — made +4.4 to +4.5 pips each and 104–177% of the net, and the other 70–80% of trades came out at −0.42 [−0.96, +0.10], −0.26 [−0.54, 0.00] and −0.04 [−0.45, +0.38]. The bid feed prints 10-pip spike bars at that minute, when real spreads on crosses are 6–12 times their normal width ([lowspreadbroker](https://lowspreadbroker-com.pages.dev/artigos/overnight-spread-widening)); fading a dealing-desk bid spike and taking profit when it reverts a minute later is a backtest artefact unless the broker actually fills limit orders there, and the vendors' own advice to avoid the 00:00–01:00 hour says they do not. The one cross that kept a positive CI outside the rollover hour was AUDNZD with a 6/12-pip limit fade (+1.03 pips [+0.39, +1.63]) and a 10/20 version (+1.62 [+0.60, +2.65]), one of seven crosses and before swap and rollover add-ons that removed half a pip; the commercial AUDNZD limit-fade EA with a live signal is at −2.42% monthly growth and its October–November 2026 reviews say it has "not been profitable for six months" and was stopped 168 pips from a 60-pip stop during an Australian CPI release ([MQL5 Signals 2367552](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/2367552); [MQL5 Market 124331](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/124331)). Night RSI(2) fades were reliably negative on all eight symbols (−1.42 pips pooled, CI [−1.50, −1.33]) because the 2×ATR night stop is 4.4 pips and costs are 47% of it.

The conditions under which the gold rule can work follow from what drove it. It needs a raw-spread ECN account where gold costs about $0.09 spread plus $3.50 per lot per side ([IC Markets](https://ic.com/global/en/trading-pricing/spreads)), because although costs are a small share of the stop, the genuine execution risk is slippage on a stop order at the London open on a trend day: 2% of our fills were gap fills averaging 52 points of adverse slippage even on minute bars, one sample break-even trade sat inside a single $54 M1 bar, independently measured worst-case gold spreads at a top ECN reached 177–255 points in mid-2025 against averages of 7–9 ([FastBull](https://www.fastbull.com/brokersview/news/september-2025-broker-spread-review-who-offers-the-best-trading-conditions-292972)), and our data is bid-only so buy stops that trigger on the ask in reality were modelled as triggering on the bid. A live spread filter in the EA (reject order placement when the spread exceeds about $0.80, retry on later ticks) is therefore a precondition rather than an edge. The server-time offset is the setting that breaks everything if wrong: the 03:00–08:00 range only means what it meant in our test on a server that is New York plus seven hours, and the EA's log line reporting server-minus-GMT must be read before anything else. News needs explicit handling: a trade opened at 08:00 is routinely still open at 13:30 server for US releases, and FTMO's funded Standard accounts prohibit "any order execution, including your Stop Loss, Take Profit or any other pending orders" within two minutes either side of Fed, NFP, GDP, FOMC-minutes and CPI releases on XAUUSD ([FTMO FAQ](https://ftmo.com/en/faq/can-i-trade-news/)), while FundingPips deducts profit from trades within five minutes of restricted events and FundedNext credits 40% of such profit ([proptradingvibes](https://www.proptradingvibes.com/blog/fundingpips-rules); [FXEmpire](https://fxempire.com/prop-firms/fundednext)); on a funded account the rule must either stay flat through those windows or accept rule-breach risk. Prop-firm drawdown limits of 5% daily and 10% total do not fit a 21.5% backtested drawdown at 1% risk; the vendor's own recommendation of 0.3–0.4% risk ([MQL5 blog 777540](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)) scales our drawdown to roughly 7–9% and the 35-month return to a few tens of percent, and FTMO's limit of 2,000 server requests a day and 200 simultaneous orders is not a constraint for a one-trade-a-day system ([FTMO](https://ftmo.com/en/forbidden-trading-practices/)). Finally, the only live account found running an Asian-range London breakout on gold in August–November 2026 is at −10.5% with PF 0.69 over 89 trades, but it mixes a long-biased H1 trend engine into a falling market, so it is evidence only that a retail implementation is not printing money in this regime ([MQL5 Signals 2387949](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/2387949)); no public result exists for the published rule itself for October–November 2026.

## Verified long-lived scalpers: 15 of 4,275, and the best-sellers keep dying

The MQL5 Signals service is the largest body of broker-fed, real-badged track records attached to MetaTrader, and on 5 November 2026 it listed about 4,275 ranked paid signals (1,608 MT4 across 34 pages, 2,667 MT5 across 56 pages), every parsed row carrying the "Real Account" badge ([MT4 by age](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/mt4/list/page1?orderby=weeks); [MT5 by age](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/mt5/list/page1?orderby=weeks)). We parsed the age-sorted lists and opened every candidate page. **378 signals have three or more years (156 weeks) of history; 332 of those show positive lifetime growth; 90 also show a drawdown of 30% or less; and 15 of the 90 hold trades for four hours or less on average — 0.35% of the universe.** Only seven combine sub-four-hour holds with at least ten trades a week (0.16%). Three of the fifteen are one vendor's news-spike accounts showing 9,000–45,000% growth on $1,000 deposits, priced at $999 a month with zero subscribers, and are outliers rather than a template ([EA Happy Gold Eightcap](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/1862128)); two more are flat or negative in money terms despite positive "growth" ([BreakoutTradeDW](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/2279070), −€42 on €500 after €2,060 of deposits). The only one of twenty requested flagship names that passed all four tests as written was TP TRADER (2,878% growth, 14,766 trades, PF 1.63, 49.4% win rate), which has 121 consecutive losses in its history, the signature of batch-closed baskets, and zero subscribers at $200 a month ([TP TRADER](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/1932739)). The 378 figure is a survivor count: the ranked lists bottom out at about −70% growth and vendors delete failed signals, so the true cohort survival rate is lower.

What the survivors have in common is more informative than their headline growth. They run on 1:200–1:500 ECN accounts (IC Markets, Tickmill, Darwinex, FP Markets, OneRoyal), trade either gold or low-volatility crosses (EURAUD, GBPCAD, AUDCAD, NZDCAD, AUDNZD), win 65–89% of trades with average losses 1.6–3.6 times average wins, run profit factors of 1.1–1.9, show relative drawdowns of 6–28%, and earn expected payoffs under $3 per trade on accounts that started with €50 to $10,000; roughly half carry deposit loads of 34–46%, the fingerprint of several concurrent positions rather than single-position scalping with tight stops ([SCR EURAUD advanced](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/2201028); [RFactor Portfolio JM](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/743742)). The headline percentages are chain-linked growth on small accounts with heavy deposit and withdrawal flows: Scalp IC Markets ECN, the archetypal night scalper with 377 weeks of history and "8,573%" growth, has earned €2,908 on €404 deposited since 2020, currently shows −3.23% monthly growth, and its reviews are dominated by copiers who lost while the master won because measured copy slippage of 0.7–1.7 pips (8–12 pips at a 20× lot multiplier) exceeds its €1.07 expected payoff per trade ([Scalp IC Markets ECN](https://www.mql5.com/en/signals/683917)). Twenty-seven of the 32 signals in our widest screen have zero subscribers, and an October 2026 audit of ten best-selling scalping and martingale EAs found every profitable linked signal running on a $50–300 account at 1:500–1:1000 for 4–54 weeks, about 80% of total profit concentrated in the first 1–7 days of each curve, and "exactly zero subscribers" on every paid copy signal in the sample ([MQL5 blog 777027](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777027)).

Two further datasets dispose of the "but the prop-firm leaderboards are full of scalpers" claim and the "just copy a winner" fallback. FundedNext's February 2026 payout report, covering $15.19M paid to 8,340 traders, shows a median of 1.5 trades a day for CFD recipients with 83.6% trading one or two times a day, a median win rate of 50% and a median profit factor of 1.47; only 415 of 10,346 paid accounts had been active more than six months ([FundedNext](https://fundednext.com/blog/fundednext-payout-report-feb-2026)). On copying, the largest academic dataset (87.9 million eToro trades) found mirrored trades averaging −61 basis points against +1 basis point for non-mirror traders, with copiers choosing by popularity rather than performance ([arXiv 2507.01817](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01817v2)); for scalping signals whose per-trade edge is one to three pips, measured copier slippage of one to two pips is the whole edge, which is why the surviving signals have no subscribers and why MQL5's own rules note the subscriber "accepts all execution risks" while the fee is kept "even if the signal blows the account in day one or two" ([MQL5 forum 386750](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/386750)).

## What realistic looks like, and how to validate any EA, including ours, in MT5

Honest numbers are modest and lumpy. Practitioner validation guidance treats a profit factor of 1.3–1.7 as the realistic band for a scalping system after costs, a PF above 2 on M1 data as a warning sign, and expects backtests without tick data and slippage to overstate live results by 30–50%, with slippage alone cutting PF by 15–25% ([Backtrex guide](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/intraday-scalping-strategy-backtesting)); the MQL5 robustness literature puts a reasonable PF at 1.2–1.5 and says anything above 1.5 "may indicate too small a sample or ignored costs" ([MQL5 Article 16957](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/16957)). Our gold rule sits inside that band at PF 1.33, with +0.13R per trade, a 44% win rate at a 2.5R target, 17 trades a month, and a 21.5% drawdown at 1% risk; the long-lived verified signals sit at 15–25% a year with 8–25% drawdowns in their good years and low single digits now. Win rates must be read against the payoff: a 55% win rate at 1:1 is break-even once a 1-pip cost is added to 10-pip stops ([LedgerMind](https://theledgermind.com/scalping-forex-complete-guide/)), and the break-even win rate for a symmetric target T with round-trip cost c is (T + c)/(2T), so a 5-pip EURUSD scalp at a realistic 2-pip all-in cost needs 70% just to tread water. Costs on raw accounts are 0.0–0.1 pip EURUSD plus $3.00–3.50 per lot per side in published terms ([IC Markets](https://ic.com/global/en/trading-accounts/raw-spread-account); [Tickmill](https://www.tickmill.com/accounts)), but independently measured averages run three to four times the published spread and broker "30 ms" execution claims measure only internal processing against 77–189 ms observed client round trips ([CompareForexBrokers](https://compareforexbrokers.com/our-methodology/execution-speeds)); broker slippage statistics are flattered by limit orders, and the orders a scalper actually cares about, stop exits, slipped negatively 57% of the time at FXCM in 2024 ([FXCM](https://www.fxcm.com/eu/execution/transparency/)). Gold's attraction is its friction-to-range ratio: about $16 per lot round trip against a $60–120 daily range in 2025–2026, roughly four to ten times cheaper per unit of movement than EURUSD, which is why gold displaced EURUSD for retail scalpers and why a spread that blows out to $2 at 08:30 ET erases a whole scalp target in seconds ([MQL5 blog 773690](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/773690)). Regulated leverage (20:1 on gold in the EU, UK and Australia) caps a $10,000 account at about half a lot, so a $1 gold scalp earns about $50 before $9–10 of costs; the 500:1 offshore and prop-firm "simulated" accounts that dominate 2026 scalping remove the cap and the protections together ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-agrees-prohibit-binary-options-and-restrict-cfds-protect-retail-investors)).

Validation in MetaTrader 5 is a sequence, and skipping any step is how vendor results are manufactured. The table gives the protocol and the evidence behind each step.

| Step | What to do in the MT5 Strategy Tester | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Data | Select **Every tick based on real ticks** on the broker server you will trade; read the real-tick percentage in the report, not "History Quality" | Generated modes build at most 11 synthetic ticks per minute and apply one fixed spread per bar; where ticks are missing the tester silently falls back to generated ticks ([MetaTrader 5 help](https://www.metatrader5.com/en/terminal/help/algotrading/tick_generation)). One breakout EA went +169, −467, −97 across the three modes ([MQL5 Article 2612](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/2612)) |
| 2. Costs | Create a custom-symbol copy with your account's commission, or enable "Use predefined commissions"; otherwise subtract trades × lots × round-turn commission by hand. Never use the "Pips" profit mode | Spread is embedded in real ticks but commission is not automatic, and Pips mode drops swap and commission entirely ([MetaTrader 5 help](https://www.metatrader5.com/en/terminal/help/algotrading/testing)) |
| 3. Delay | Set **Fixed Delay** to your measured ping times 1.5–2 and compare with 0 ms | A gold tick-scalping study found its only surviving edge died above about 50 ms ([MQL5 blog 777925](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777925)); the one matched live-vs-backtest comparison found a 20% shortfall, 75% of it from fill prices and none from commission ([MQL5 blog 777157](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777157)) |
| 4. Spread stress | Re-run in 1-minute-OHLC mode with a fixed pessimistic spread (25–35 points on 2-digit gold, 8–12 on EURUSD) and with the EA's spread filter widened | An EA profitable at a fixed 10-point spread lost under variable spread because widening at news, opens and rollover ate the trades that barely reached target ([MQL5 forum 212322](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/212322/page2)) |
| 5. Sample size | Treat fewer than 300 trades as no information; a noisy scalper needs about 4 × (SD of trade / mean trade)² trades, typically 500, then doubled for biases; run each calendar year separately | n = (t / per-trade Sharpe)² ([EliteTrader](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/minimum-number-of-trades-required-for-backtesting-results-to-be-trusted.356588/page-2)); our gold rule's 2024–2025 years each had CIs through zero on 194–222 trades |
| 6. Hold-out | Use Forward 1/3 or a custom date; optimise at most 3–4 parameters with coarse steps; pick the middle of a plateau, never a spike; count every optimiser pass as a trial | Five years of daily data support no more than about 45 trials before the best result is expected to be a fluke; an in-sample Sharpe of 1.59 produced −0.18 out of sample ([Bailey et al.](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/overfit-tools-at.pdf)). MT5's forward test is a single hold-out, not a rolling walk-forward ([MetaTrader 5 help](https://www.metatrader5.com/en/terminal/help/algotrading/strategy_optimization)) |
| 7. Benchmarks | Compare against random direction at the same entries and against buy-and-hold or a long-only version | Our random-direction benchmark averaged −41 points against the rule's +283; passive gold matched the vendor's +137% over 2023–2026 ([WGC Dec 2022](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-market-commentary-december-2022); [WGC Sep 2026](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-market-commentary-september-2026)) |
| 8. Server time | Attach the EA to a demo chart and read its "server time minus GMT" log line; shift every hour input accordingly; test adjacent windows | On a UTC+2/+3 server, 08:00 UK is 10:00 server; our 04:00–09:00 window was never significant while 03:00–08:00 was |
| 9. Forward test | At least three months on a demo at the intended broker, then at least three months at the smallest lot; match each live trade to its backtest twin; fix halt rules in advance (live drawdown beyond the backtest maximum, fill shortfall beyond the modelled slippage, missing-trade rate) | "Forward test on a live demo with your intended broker... months, not days" ([MQL5 blog 765944](https://www.mql5.com/es/blogs/post/765944)); trade-level reconciliation method ([MQL5 blog 777157](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777157)) |
| 10. Position size | Verify the first fills by hand: volume × contract size × stop distance should equal balance × risk percent | The published version of this strategy once opened positions about 10× too large on one broker through a tick-value mismatch ([MQL5 blog 777540](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)) |

To make the one surviving candidate testable on your own broker, this research produced an MT5 Expert Advisor, `LondonRangeBreakout.mq5`, in the `mql5/` folder of the research notes, with a README, a desk-review log and three presets ([README](../research_notes/MetaTrader%20scalping%20strategies%202026/mql5/README.md); [REVIEW](../research_notes/MetaTrader%20scalping%20strategies%202026/mql5/REVIEW.md)). It is a research tool, claims no win rate, profit factor or drawdown, and uses no DLLs, file I/O or network calls. Each server-time day it builds the range from completed M1 bars (or chart-timeframe bars) between configurable start and end times, skips the day if the range is outside configurable percent-of-price or point limits, places OCO buy and sell stops with a point buffer at the trade-start hour (or, in an alternative mode, enters at market on a close beyond the range by a multiple of ATR), puts the stop at the far side of the range or at an ATR multiple with a percent-of-price cap, sets the target as a multiple of the stop distance, moves to break-even at a configurable R, optionally trails by ATR, deletes unfilled stops at the trade-end hour, closes everything at the force-close hour, enforces one fill per day, refuses to place orders when the spread exceeds a point limit or inside static news-blackout windows, and sizes each trade from a risk percentage of balance using `OrderCalcProfit` with a tick-value cross-check that logs a warning if the two disagree by more than 20%. The preset `LondonRangeBreakout_XAUUSD_exact_tested.set` reproduces the configuration we backtested: range 03:00–08:00 server, orders at 08:00, orders deleted and positions closed at 21:00, buffer 20 points, range filter 0.30–1.60% of price, stop at the far side capped at 0.60%, 2.5R target, break-even at 1.5R, one trade a day, 1% risk, spread cap 80 points, hours assuming a server on New York time plus seven hours. Two honesty points about the EA itself: its author had no MetaEditor, so the source is desk-reviewed line by line rather than compiled, and the review log records eight logic fixes (stale positions and pending orders from a previous day being counted as today's, a partial chart-timeframe bar straddling the range end, a fill-count gap when both stops could fill on a hedging account, and others) plus a short list of header-version items to confirm in MetaEditor; and our backtest filled stop orders on bid-only minute data, whereas the EA's buy stops will trigger on the ask and fill with real slippage, so a real-tick run on your broker is a different and harder test than ours. Compile it, run steps 1 through 10 above with the exact-tested preset, read the Journal's range, fill and exit lines for a few days to confirm it does what you think, then run 2024, 2025 and 2026 separately and ask whether you would have kept running it through the 416 trades of 2024–2025 that our data could not distinguish from zero. If the result does not survive commission, a realistic delay and a pessimistic spread on your server, it does not survive.

## Conclusion

The question "what works 100 percent" has a precise answer in the data: nothing, and the systems that appear to are the ones most certain to fail, because a 99% basket win rate is purchased with a one-percent event that costs the account. The question "what is actually working in November 2026" has a narrower and more interesting answer than the marketing suggests. The one rule that passes an independent, cost-inclusive, random-benchmarked test is not a scalp in the five-pip sense at all; it is a once-a-day volatility capture on gold with a wide range-based stop and a 2.5R target, and its edge is a property of a gold market whose Asian range quadrupled in three years. The evidence shift since the 2018–2022 generation of "night scalpers" is from microstructure edges, which the spread has absorbed, to regime edges, which the spread cannot touch but which the regime can withdraw at any time. A trader who runs the gold London-range rule should do so knowing that two of its three years were statistically flat, that the best 5% of trades carry the whole result, and that the next calm year is the test it has not yet passed.

Two implications follow. First, the useful unit of evidence in this market is no longer a backtest or a signal page but the pair of them reconciled trade by trade on your own broker, with explicit commission, measured delay and a pessimistic spread; the research tooling to do that is free, and every vendor who declines to publish those three settings is telling you something. Second, position sizing, not entry timing, is where the realistic money is managed: at the 0.3–0.4% risk that fits a prop-firm drawdown limit, the best-evidenced scalping-timeframe rule in existence compounds to a few tens of percent over three years with single-digit drawdowns, which is a defensible, modest business and a very long way from passive income.
