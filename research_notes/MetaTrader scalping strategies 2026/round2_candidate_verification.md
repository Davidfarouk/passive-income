## Key Question 1: Gold (XAUUSD) London-open / Asian-range breakout — exact published rules, author identity and commercial interest, cost assumptions, in-sample vs hold-out, forward/live status

### Takeaway
The only gold London-range breakout with a multi-year, real-tick, hold-out-reported test (MQL5 blog 777540, 13 Oct 2026) is a 03:00–08:00-server-time Asian-range OCO stop-order system with a 2.5R target, break-even at 1.5R, a 0.30–1.60%-of-price range filter and a 21:00 flat rule; its author published it three days after listing the identical EA for sale at $59, the spread/commission settings of the test are nowhere stated, the "hold-out" is Jan–Sep 2026 (157 trades), and the promised demo forward-test results are posted only in a buyers-only comments tab. The separate "0.15×ATR close / 0.8×ATR SL / 1.5R / 80-point spread cap" variant (MQL5 blog 767326, 11 Feb 2026) is a code blueprint from a toolkit vendor with **no** backtest at all; the two variants should not be conflated.

- MQL5 documentation: "During testing, the spread is not modeled but is taken from historical data." ### Inferences
- Because MT5 real-tick mode embeds the broker's historical bid/ask, the undisclosed item in 777540 is almost certainly **commission and execution delay**, not spread: unless the author enabled "Use predefined commissions" or a custom commission (nothing says he did), the test is spread-inclusive but commission-free and delay-free. On a raw account (IC Markets $7/lot round turn = $0.07/oz) that omission is small; on the "Broker A/B" standard accounts the wider spread is already in the ticks.
- The blog's 0.5%-risk drawdown (11.2%) and the product page's "about 9%" for the same run do not match, and the product's $39/$59/$79 price statements conflict — small inconsistencies, but they come from the only source of the result.
- "Settings chosen on 2023–2025 data only" is a single train/test split, not a walk-forward; with ~10 free parameters (range hours, buffer, two range-filter thresholds, SL cap, TP multiple, BE trigger, exit hour, Monday/Friday switches, spread filter) and a 157-trade hold-out, a PF of 1.61 is within the range that selection on the 2023–2025 sample alone could produce.
- The sale of the EA three days before the "research" post, and the restriction of forward results to a buyers-only tab, make this a vendor-authored backtest, not an independent study.

### Gaps
- No year-by-year table for 2023, 2024, 2025 (only the aggregate and the 2026 hold-out); no long/short split; no average win/loss in R; no statement of whether the 715 trades include break-even exits as "wins".
- The buffer size, GMT offset, broker identities and tester commission/delay settings remain unknown; no reader has yet posted a replication.
- No demo or live equity curve for Oct–Nov 2026 is publicly readable.
- Variant B (0.15×ATR / 0.8×ATR / 1.5R / 80-point cap) has **zero** published test results of any kind; the figures attached to it in earlier summaries actually belong to Variant A.

## Key Question 2: How robust is the gold London-range result to explicit spread + commission and across years, and does the same rule hold on EURUSD / GBPUSD / indices?

### Takeaway
No source re-runs the gold rule with explicit per-ounce costs, so robustness can only be bounded arithmetically: a 26–42-point round trip is roughly 1–7% of a range-based stop under the author's own 0.30–1.60% range filter, which would trim an expectancy of roughly 0.1R per trade by perhaps a tenth to a half at the narrow end — large enough to matter, not large enough to flip the sign on its own. Every attempt to run the same Asian-range/London-breakout logic on EURUSD and GBPUSD is flat to negative (Backtrex PF 0.87, cTrader PF 1.03, GBPUSD M15 −60%), and the index version survives gross but not net of CFD costs (KQ3), so the gold result stands alone and is regime-specific.

### Cited Findings
- Other vendors' gold Asian-range breakout tests cluster at the same level: "London Gold Breakout" (Roman Mudarisov, 13 May 2026, 50 USD; XAUUSD M15, every tick, 2024-01-01 to 2026-05-11, $10,000, preset "Balanced DD+"): net +$16,221.81 (+162.2%), PF 1.33, equity DD 19.42%, 435 trades; **spread/commission settings not stated**, win rate not stated, no live signal, no reviews; entry type, range window and stop/target rules are not disclosed on the page — [MQL5 Market 176885](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/176885)
- A search-snippet version of the same listing cites PF 1.36, max DD 14.7%, 419 trades with SL behind the opposite side of the range and TP 1.5× range width — inconsistent with the page read directly (PF 1.33 / 19.42% / 435) — [MQL5 Market 176885 (search snippet)](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/176885)
- Independent (non-vendor) analysis of gold daily range from IC Markets MT5 D1 data, 2 Jan 2020–22 Oct 2026: average daily range 2023 $23.67 (1.22% of price), 2024 $33.02 (1.38%), 2025 $60.70 (1.73%), 2026 to 22 Oct $120.50 (2.63%; median $100.03 / 2.20%); first close of 2024 2,058.91; close 22 Oct 2026 4,024.32; author Lewis Kosasih lists no products — [MQL5 blog 778649](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/778649)
- Spread/commission reference (prior round): IC Markets Raw average XAUUSD spread 0.09 (9 points) plus $3.50/lot/side ($7 round turn = $0.07/oz); trader-reported standard-account spreads "around 20–25 points" in Asian hours; FastBull monthly rankings show IC Markets average 7.3–8.5 points but worst 177–255 points in Jul–Sep 2025 — [IC Markets](https://ic.com/global/en/trading-pricing/spreads/); [MQL5 blog 770439](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/770439); [FastBull Sep 2025](https://www.fastbull.com/brokersview/news/brokersview-july-2025-spread-comparison-finding-the-best-trading-conditions-267860)
- A gold-EA vendor who re-tested on real ticks for a year concludes that "tight-stop breakouts and thin-TP scalps are punished on real ticks by spread/slippage", that "narrow session filters decay fastest", and that buy-side logic "looked strong only because of the post-2023 uptrend" (prior round) — [MQL5 blog 774275](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/774275)
- Same logic on GBPUSD (cTrader cBot "GBPUSD London Breakout", author hermanl, seller since 3 Mar 2026; H1; pre-London consolidation range, confirmed breakout in a window, daily EMA trend filter, fixed-R target, buffered stop, BE): backtest 01/01/2024–16/08/2026 on FxPro conditions, 213 trades, 55.4% win, **PF 1.03**, ROI 22.35%, max DD ~47–49% at 5% risk; spread/commission "FxPro conditions" only; no reviews — [cTrader product 5168](https://ctrader.com/products/5168)
- Same logic on EUR/USD (Backtrex, H1 London breakout, 2 Oct 2016–1 Oct 2026, 1,230 trades, commission 0.02%/side, spread/slippage not stated): −28% total, CAGR −3.2%, PF 0.87, max DD −31.4%; GBP/USD M15 variant −60.0%, loss in every calendar year (prior round) — [Backtrex EUR/USD London breakout](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/london-breakout-eur-usd)
- Same logic on index CFDs: see KQ3 (gross +0.05 to +0.13R, net ≤ +0.002R) — [MQL5 blog 776235](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- Cost-share benchmark from the index replication: "0.1 R is what a round trip of 2.5 points costs on a 25-point stop" and on the median NQ day since 2021 the stop is 39 points from entry, so a 2.5-point round trip is 6% of risk, 12% on SPX and FTSE — [KruegerAlgorithms PDF](https://kruegeralgorithms.com/research/opening-range-breakout-paper-replication/Opening_Range_Breakout_Paper_Replication.pdf)
- A 13-year gold channel-breakout test with hard stops (Sotirios Karkalakos, 18 Sep 2026; sells "Gold Channel Rider"): 2013–2026 continuous path, win 50.7%, PF 1.75, +298%, CAGR 10.6%, max DD 9.3%, 13 of 14 years positive, 2022 −5.2%; rolling 1-month windows profitable 52%, 3-month 68%, 12-month 88%; **timeframe, channel definition, data/tick mode and cost model not stated** — [MQL5 blog 776032](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776032)
- A 4-year M1 gold pivot-breakout EA test (Kenichiro Sakamoto, 3 Aug 2026; Dukascopy real ticks, 2022–2025, $10,000, 1% risk): PF 1.30, ~+5.1%/yr compounded, max equity DD 6.4%, 86.8% win over 1,022 trades; author states "the same strategy family lost in 2018–2021", "no configuration was found that wins in all regimes" and there is "no live forward record yet"; spread/commission not stated — [MQL5 blog 773562](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/773562)

### Inferences
- Cost arithmetic for the gold rule (my calculation, flagged): with the author's range filter, the stop (= range, capped at 0.6% of price) spans about $6–$12 per oz at 2023 prices (~$2,000) and $12–$24 at 2026 prices (~$4,000). A $0.26–0.42 round trip is therefore 2.2–7.0% of R on the narrowest allowed 2023 days and 1.1–3.5% of R at the 0.6% cap in 2026. The system's realised expectancy is roughly $9.6 per trade on an average R of ~$80 (1% of equity rising from $5,000 to ~$11,860), i.e. ~0.12R. If spread is already embedded (MT5 real ticks) and only commission (~$0.07/oz raw) is missing, the omission is ~0.5–1% of R and PF 1.38 would fall to roughly 1.35; if a reader instead runs it on a standard account where the spread is 20–25 points *wider* than the test broker's, the extra 2–4% of R per trade would remove roughly a sixth to a third of the expectancy. Nothing in the source allows a tighter statement.
- The 2.5R target with break-even at 1.5R makes the result much less cost-sensitive than a thin-target scalp — most of the published "scalps" in this folder die at 0.1R costs because their targets are 0.5–1R.
- The same Asian-range/London logic has now been tested on EURUSD (PF 0.87), GBPUSD (PF 1.03 and −60%), five index CFDs (net ≈0) and gold (PF 1.33–1.38 by two vendors over overlapping 2023/2024–2026 windows): the only positive instrument is the one that trended hardest in the window. That pattern is what a regime effect looks like, not an instrument-agnostic microstructure edge.

### Gaps
- No source recomputes the gold London-range result with explicit per-ounce costs, by year, or with execution delay; the arithmetic above is an inference, not a finding.
- The two gold vendors' rule sets differ in unstated ways (buffer, range hours, TP), so their similar PFs are not an independent replication.
- The Karkalakos 13-year test has the broadest regime coverage of any gold breakout result but discloses neither its rules nor its cost model, so it cannot be used to validate the London-range rule.

## Key Question 3: Index opening-range breakout — exact cost and leverage assumptions in Zarattini & Aziz (2023) and Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz (2024), and what independent replications on CFDs/futures conclude

### Takeaway
Both papers charge commission only ($0.0005/share in 2023; $0.0035/share IBKR Pro Tiered in 2024), assume fills at the recorded price with "no slippage", size every trade to 1% risk under a 4× leverage cap on a $25,000 account, and test one window (2016–Feb 2023 / 2016–Dec 2023) with no hold-out; the 2024 paper's phrase "even after considering transaction costs" refers to commissions alone — the word "slippage" does not occur in it. The only cost-inclusive independent replication (KruegerAlgorithms, Sep 2026) reproduces the gross +0.13R on NQ CFD data to the second decimal and finds net +0.002R on NQ and −0.04 to −0.08R on SPX, Dow, DAX and FTSE; the futures test with the best out-of-sample Sharpe (1.10, MNQ 2023–Feb 2026) charges commission but no slippage and underperforms buy-and-hold in that window.

### Inferences
- The two academic papers' cost model is identical in kind — commissions only, fills at the printed price — and the 2023 paper itself concedes that the no-slippage assumption makes its tighter-stop variant "unrealistic"; the base case has, as Krueger puts it, "the same problem in a milder dose". The 2024 paper's reliance on **stop orders at the 5-minute extreme of the day's most active stocks** is exactly the execution regime where slippage is largest, and it is unmodelled.
- A MetaTrader CFD trader is closer to Krueger's conditions (CFD spread, 24-hour instrument, no 4× sizing) than to either paper's; the best available net estimate for that trader is ≈0R on NQ and negative elsewhere.
- The MNQ result is the only ORB test with a Sharpe above 1 out of sample, but it charges no slippage, optimised two parameters on the in-sample Sharpe, and still trailed buy-and-hold in a +127% window; its edge is a lower-drawdown, lower-exposure return stream, not alpha in excess of the index.

### Gaps
- Neither paper publishes calendar-year returns, so a year-by-year comparison against the CFD replication is impossible from the texts.
- No independent, cost-inclusive replication of the 2024 "Stocks in Play" rule over 2016–2026 was found (QuantConnect shows 2016 only; Krueger explicitly did not test it).
- The Backtests-Not-Signals opening-range length, slippage assumption and per-year table are paywalled.
- The SSRN pages themselves were not fetched (the PDFs were taken from the authors' site, which carries the Sep 2025 revision of the 2023 paper and the Feb 2024 first version of the 2024 paper; the 29 Apr 2025 SSRN revision of the latter was not checked for changes).
