## Key Question 1: What are the concrete rule sets for the popular versions (Asian-range London breakout, London/NY ORB, Silver Bullet, index ORB, XAUUSD 2024–2026 variants)?

### Takeaway
There is no single canonical definition; every published variant is a box (Asian range or first 5/15/30/60 min of a session) plus a buffer, a stop on the far side or mid-range, a fixed-R or measured-move target, and a hard time cutoff. The best-documented concrete rule sets with attached test results are the MQL5 gold London-range EA (03:00–08:00 server range, OCO stops, SL = far side capped at 0.6%, TP 2.5R, BE at 1.5R, close 21:00, skip ranges <0.30% or >1.60%), the Zarattini–Aziz QQQ 5-minute ORB (direction of first 5-min candle, stop at its far side, 10R target or close), and the ICT Silver Bullet (10:00–11:00 NY, liquidity sweep → displacement FVG → entry in the gap, stop beyond the sweep, target opposing liquidity).

### Cited Findings

**Asian-range breakout at London open (forex and gold)**

**Opening-range breakout on indices (US30/NAS100/DAX/QQQ)**
- Zarattini & Aziz (2023) QQQ 5-minute ORB: if QQQ rises (falls) in the first 5-minute bar, buy (short) at the open of the second bar; no trade if open ≈ close (doji); stop at the low (long) / high (short) of the first 5-min bar; profit target = 10× the entry-to-stop distance; otherwise liquidate at the close; each trade sized so a stop costs 1% of capital; leverage capped at 4x; a TQQQ variant uses a stop of 5% of 14-day ATR, no target, close exit — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/day-trading-with-an-opening-range-breakout-strategy); [SSRN 4416622](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4416622)
- Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz (2024) "Stocks in Play" ORB: universe = US stocks with open > $5, 14-day ADV ≥ 1,000,000 shares, 14-day ATR > $0.50, opening-range relative volume ≥ 100% vs prior 14 days, top-20 each day; stop orders long above / short below the first 5-minute range; stop-loss 10% of ATR from entry; close at market close; sized so a stop loses 1% of capital; 4x leverage cap — [CXO Advisory summary (3 Apr 2024)](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/intraday-trading-of-overactive-stocks-via-opening-range-breakout/)

- Backtests-Not-Signals MNQ ORB: long above/short below the US opening range; daily SMA(200) trend filter (longs only above, shorts only below); stop = far side of the range capped at 150 ticks (~$75); TP = R-multiple of stop (grid 1.4–4.4); forced exit at next session open; stop trading for the session after one loss — [Backtests, Not Signals (Substack)](https://backtestsnotsignals.substack.com/p/opening-range-breakout-real-edge)

**ICT Silver Bullet**
- Three windows in New York time: 03:00–04:00 (London), 10:00–11:00 (NY AM), 14:00–15:00 (NY PM); before the window map liquidity (session highs/lows, equal highs/lows, unfilled imbalances); wait for a sweep of one pool or a structure shift; enter only on a fair value gap created by displacement inside the window, positioning inside the gap; stop beyond the manipulation swing (tighter: beyond far edge of the gap); target the opposing pool; if no valid gap forms before the window closes, no trade; "no audited public statistics exist" — [LuxAlgo Silver Bullet concept page](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/silver-bullet.md)
- Vendor version (TakeProfit, affiliate-linked): daily/4H bias set before the window; sweep of a recent high/low; displacement FVG on M5/M15 of 15–20 pips (EURUSD) or 30+ ticks (NAS100); retest entry at the 50% of the FVG within 30–90 min; stop beyond the sweep (10–20 pips EURUSD, 30–60 ticks NAS100); RR 1:2 to 1:4 — [TakeProfit guide](https://takeprofitapp.com/en/learn/ict-silver-bullet-strategy)

### Inferences
- The two structural differences that most change results are (a) stop location (far side of range vs mid-range vs tight ATR) and (b) whether entry is a stop order at the edge or a confirmed close beyond it. The MQL5 gold test explicitly found mid-range/tight stops were "hit too often by normal noise"; the TradingView Classic script (mid-range stop, 1:1) is the one its own author says failed over 100+ trades.
- All server-time rules (03:00–08:00, 18:35, 16:30) are broker-specific; several sources (MQL5 gold blog, Lee Samson, NAS100 ORB EA listing) warn that DST and GMT-offset misalignment silently breaks both backtests and live sessions.

### Gaps
- No source gives a single, widely agreed buffer size for the Asian-range breakout; values range from 3–5 pips to 0.15 ATR to "small" (undisclosed in the only tested gold version).
- Commercial XAUUSD EAs (London Gold Breakout, AurumBreak Pro, Gold Breakout King) do not disclose exact session windows, buffers or stop formulas; only marketing-level rule descriptions are public.
- Could not retrieve the ForexFactory "London Open Breakout" thread rule set (search returned no 2025 ForexFactory pages; forum fetches were blocked).

## Key Question 2: What backtests and academic evidence exist, and what do they show (win rate, PF, Sharpe, drawdown, cost sensitivity)?

### Takeaway
The academic ORB evidence (Zarattini & Aziz 2023: 33% CAGR on QQQ 2016–2023; Zarattini–Barbon–Aziz 2024: 41.6% annualised, Sharpe 2.81 on Stocks-in-Play) is gross of spread and slippage, has no out-of-sample period, and an independent 2015–2026 replication on five index CFDs reproduced the gross +0.13R/trade but found net R indistinguishable from zero after realistic CFD costs. For forex, the only rigorous independent London-breakout test found (Backtrex EUR/USD H1, 2016–2026) is a loser (PF 0.87, −28%), while the one MT5 real-tick gold test (2023–2026) shows PF 1.37–1.38 with 15–19% drawdowns at 1% risk and PF 1.61 on a 2026 hold-out. Time-of-day FX return seasonality is real (Breedon & Ranaldo; Ranaldo 2009) but tradeable after spread only in EUR/USD at Sharpe ≈1 using interdealer prices.

**Academic time-of-day and intraday-momentum evidence (dated)**

### Inferences
- The single most important pattern across all tests: gross ORB edge on indices is real but small (≈0.05–0.13R per trade), and CFD spread+slippage of ≈0.1R per trade consumes it. The MNQ futures test (commission-only, no slippage) retains a Sharpe ≈1.1 OOS; the CFD replication (spread+slippage) does not. A MetaTrader CFD trader should therefore expect results closer to the MQL5 replication than to the academic paper.
- The MQL5 gold result (PF ~1.37, ~49% win, 2.5R target, 2023–2026) is the most relevant positive evidence for an MT5 trader, but its spread assumptions are undisclosed, it is vendor-authored, and it has no live track record; the same author warns the edge "needs hundreds of trades to show."

## Key Question 4: News scalping (NFP, CPI, FOMC, ECB) — straddles, slippage, spread widening, broker execution, prop-firm restrictions. Is it net profitable for retail?

### Takeaway
No source — academic, broker, prop-firm or trader — provides a cost-inclusive track record showing retail straddle/pending-order news scalping is net profitable; the quantified evidence that exists is about costs (gold spread 22→108 points in under a minute on NFP; 60–140-point spreads for 3–5 minutes at 13:30 UTC; 5–8 points of latency slippage), and the academic literature shows the initial jump is fast and efficient (Andersen et al. 2003) with a *reversal* of ~65% of pre-FOMC drift 12–24 hours later in FX (Lee & Wang 2025) rather than exploitable minute-scale drift. FTMO bans any order execution (including SL/TP) within ±2 minutes of listed events on funded Standard accounts; other firms range from unrestricted (FundedNext) to "prohibited" (The5ers, per third-party rule page).

### Cited Findings

**Academic evidence on announcement reactions**
- Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold & Vega, "Micro Effects of Macro Announcements: Real-Time Price Discovery in Foreign Exchange" (AER 2003, 93(1), 38–62; NBER WP 8959, 2002): six years of 5-minute returns (DEM, GBP, JPY, CHF, EUR vs USD; 2,189 days, >630,000 observations); announcement *surprises* (actual minus survey expectation) produce conditional-mean jumps; "bad news has a greater impact than good news"; more timely releases have the largest impact; US news dominates, only two German indicators moved DEM/USD — [SSRN 313660](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=313660); [NBER w8959 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w8959/w8959.pdf)
- Lee & Wang, "Jumps and Post-FOMC Announcement Returns in Currency Markets" (Review of Asset Pricing Studies 2025, 15(3–4), 247–287): high-frequency FX data; positive pre-FOMC drift; post-FOMC returns are "significantly low, cancelling out approximately 65% of positive pre-FOMC announcement drifts"; reversals realised mostly 12–24 hours after the announcement, tied to negative jump volatility and uncertainty resolution — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rasset/v15y2025i3-4p247-287..html)
- Borsa Istanbul Review (Jan 2022) high-frequency FOMC/exchange-rate study (abstract via search only; page 403): exchange rates respond strongly to expansionary announcements and weakly to tighter/unchanged policy — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/91ee4c3f98784d39bae5a656fe5bc5c7)
- Gao et al. (2018): intraday momentum (first half-hour → last half-hour) is *stronger* on days with major macroeconomic news releases — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414)

**Spread widening, slippage and execution (trader/EA-developer reports, 2022–2026)**
- XAUUSD live vs fixed-spread backtest: backtest at fixed 18-point spread; live spread in the 13:30 UTC data window "60, 90, and sometimes 140 points, lasting 3 to 5 minutes"; March 2022 NFP: spread rose from 22 to 108 points in under a minute; a calm Tuesday 14:52 UTC spread ~110 points with no catalyst; a VPS 30–40 ms slower than co-located clients produced entries 5–8 points worse than signal price; a routing hop adding 55 ms per execution identified as the cause of poor fills; MT5 Strategy Tester "does not simulate spread at execution, order queue depth, or partial fills"; recommendations: variable-spread backtests, pre-order spread check with abort threshold, pause around high-impact news, demo ≥60 sessions, track slippage as its own line item — [MQL5 blog 770488](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/770488)
- Financial Source (5 Oct 2024) on why straddles fail (qualitative, no figures): slippage when liquidity dries up, whipsaw (spike triggers entry then reverses into stop), brokers widening spreads/restricting trading, both orders triggering with no directional bias, cost accumulation; recommends instead trading continuation only when actual beats the *high* or misses the *low* forecast, entering within 30 seconds, stop below the spike candle, 30–100-pip targets (untested) — [Financial Source](https://financialsource.co/en/why-straddle-news-trading-doesnt-work)
- Forum/educational snippets (pages 403 on fetch; treat as anecdotal): spreads on NFP "can become very large" and widen *before* the release; a widely repeated but unverified figure of 8–10-pip spreads with fills 5–10 pips worse than expected; traders report both straddle legs filling on a spike leaving a hedged position; stops slipping past levels; mitigations suggested = 15–20-pip order spacing, ECN/STP broker, partial size, wait for first post-release candle — [BabyPips: Widening of spread during news](https://forums.babypips.com/t/widening-ofspread-during-news/35968); [BabyPips: Trading the News NFP](https://forums.babypips.com/t/trading-the-news-nfp-this-afternoon/189520); [ForexPeaceArmy: filter to avoid huge slippage](https://www.forexpeacearmy.com/community/threads/filter-to-avoid-huge-slippage.37333/post-185818); [thenexttrade (unverified)](https://thenexttrade.vercel.app/academy/lesson/non-directional-trade-profiting-from-volatility-not-direction)

### Inferences
- The structural problem for a MetaTrader straddle is that both legs are *stop* orders filled at market during the exact seconds when spread is 3–6× normal (gold 22→108 points) and latency slippage is largest; a 2:1 bracket sized to normal spreads is routinely destroyed by the fill alone. No source contradicts this.
- The academic jump literature implies the first move is informationally efficient within the first 5-minute bar; the documented exploitable patterns are either pre-announcement drift (requires holding into the event, which prop firms restrict) or 12–24-hour reversal (not a scalp).
- For funded-account traders, the FTMO SL/TP clause means a session-breakout trade opened at 08:00 London that is still open at 13:30 NFP (e.g. the MQL5 gold EA's 21:00 cutoff) is itself a rule-breach risk unless the EA removes or avoids SL/TP execution in the window — a practical reason the session-breakout and news families conflict on prop accounts.

### Gaps
- No academic or broker study quantifying average retail spread/slippage during NFP/CPI/FOMC for EUR/USD, GBP/USD or XAUUSD in 2024–2026 was found; all figures are single-trader anecdotes.
- No cost-inclusive backtest or verified live record of a news-straddle or post-news continuation strategy was found (Financial Source's method is untested; alphatradecircle "slippage math" page was judged low quality and not used).
- FundingPips' and FundedNext's *official* news pages could not be retrieved (search budget exhausted); third-party descriptions conflict (5-minute Master-account window and profit deduction vs "Allowed").
- No CPI- or ECB-specific high-frequency FX studies were retrieved.

## Key Question 6: What filters are reported to improve breakout scalps (ATR/volatility, trend, time cutoff, tick volume, daily bias), and are there quantified comparisons?

### Takeaway
Quantified comparisons are rare and mostly on index futures, not forex: a trend-direction filter raised profit-to-drawdown by +53% on E-mini NASDAQ hourly breakouts (2021–2026, vendor research); a daily SMA-200 filter plus one-loss-per-session rule gave a Sharpe-1.1 out-of-sample MNQ ORB; a range-width filter (0.30–1.60% of price) and break-even at 1.5R were the keepers in the gold London test; and the largest Silver Bullet study found the liquidity-sweep requirement was the only rule that consistently added edge while 15-minute bias/MSS filters hurt. Tick-volume filters are described as weak in MT5 forex and no quantified test was found.

### Cited Findings
- Trend-direction filter (entry bar vs today's open) on E-mini NASDAQ 60-min breakouts, 2021–2026: average trade $120 → $162 (+35%), max DD ~$40k → ~$25k (−37%), profit-to-drawdown 5.24 → ~8.0 (+53%), "zero optimizable parameters"; time segmentation excluding pre-market +8% avg trade; after-market only +19%; ATR-bin volatility timing on Nikkei 225 avg trade $160 → $306 and DD −79% but net profit −6% with trades roughly halved; author warns "every parameter you add to a strategy is a degree of freedom" and that "the more precise your filter looks in the backtest, the more likely it is capturing noise"; commissions/slippage not quantified; vendor blog (Mar 2026) — [BreakoutOS research (vendor)](https://breakoutos-com.pages.dev/blog/research-false-breakout-filters)
- Daily SMA(200) trend filter + stop-after-one-loss + 150-tick stop cap on MNQ ORB: OOS 2023–Feb 2026 PF 1.32, Sharpe 1.10, win 24.9%; optimising for win rate instead of Sharpe pushed win rate above 40% but lowered Sharpe — [Backtests, Not Signals](https://backtestsnotsignals.substack.com/p/opening-range-breakout-real-edge)
- Gold London breakout filters that survived: range filter skip <0.30% or >1.60% of price, SL at far side capped at 0.6%, BE at 1.5R, TP 2.5R, 21:00 cutoff; rejected: mid-range stops, no range filter, overnight holds, 5R target (PF 1.30) — [MQL5 blog 777540](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)
- MQL5 USTEC ORB: a 350-period SMA filter "roughly halved trades" and "suits a regime filter"; VWAP signals worked better on M15 than M1 on CFDs, attributed to costs — [MQL5 article 17745](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/17745)

### Inferences
- The filters with the best evidence-to-parameter ratio are parameter-light: trend direction relative to the open or a long daily MA, a range-width band relative to price/ATR, one trade per session, and a hard time cutoff. Volume/tick-volume and complex intraday bias rules have either no quantified support or negative results.
- Because every filter cuts trade count (often by half), and the base edge is ~0.05–0.13R, filtered systems need multi-year samples (hundreds of trades) to distinguish edge from noise — exactly what the 2025–2026 Reddit backtests lack.

### Gaps
- No quantified forex-specific (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, XAUUSD) comparison of ATR vs trend vs volume filters on a London breakout was found; the +53% trend-filter figure is on NQ hourly breakouts, not session ORB.
- No test of a "daily bias" filter (e.g., previous-day direction or HTF structure) with published numbers on forex session breakouts.
- QuantifiedStrategies' and Quantpedia's filter studies could not be retrieved.
