# Independent, quantitative evidence on retail forex/CFD scalping profitability (academic studies, rigorous backtests, transaction-cost analysis) — relevance to MT4/MT5 traders 2025-2026

Research notes compiled 2026-11-05. Method: 18 distinct web searches, ~45 page/PDF fetches, five full academic PDFs converted to text (Krohn-Mueller-Whelan 2021; Breedon-Ranaldo 2008 draft; Neely-Weller-Ulrich 2007; Barber-Lee-Liu-Odean 2011; CMC Markets Canada disclosures). The shared web-search budget was exhausted mid-task, so the last ~10 planned searches (Robot Wealth, Alpha Architect, FCA statistics, TradingView strategy reports, Asian-session mean-reversion backtests, news-trading backtests) could not be run; those appear in the Gaps sections. Where sources conflict or are promotional, that is flagged inline.

Conventions: "RT" = round trip; "PF" = profit factor; "WR" = win rate; "BA" = bid-ask spread; "SR" = Sharpe ratio; "OOS" = out-of-sample.

## KQ1. What do peer-reviewed / working-paper studies say about intraday and technical-rule profitability in FX after costs?

### Takeaway
The academic record is consistent across four decades of papers: simple technical rules (filters, MA crossovers, channel breakouts) earned genuine net excess returns in developed-market FX in the 1970s-80s, but those returns collapsed to roughly zero by the early 1990s and have not come back for majors; what survives is (a) emerging-market currencies and (b) intraday time-of-day / fixing-reversal patterns whose gross returns are large and highly significant but which are "usually more than offset by transaction costs" at quoted spreads, becoming positive only at ~half the indicative spread. Intraday studies that use real rather than estimated costs (Frömmel & Lampaert 2016; Marshall et al. 2008 for equities) find higher-frequency rules show more raw predictability but that real costs "wipe away most of the profits".

### Cited Findings

**Daily technical rules in FX — long-run decay**
- Neely, Weller & Ulrich (St. Louis Fed WP 2006-046B, rev. Mar 2007; published JFQA 44(2), 2009): "The excess returns of the 1970s and 1980s were genuine and not just the result of data mining. But these profit opportunities had disappeared by the early 1990s for filter and moving average rules. Returns to less-studied rules also have declined but have probably not completely disappeared." — [St. Louis Fed PDF](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2006/2006-046.pdf); [JFQA record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:jfinqa:v:44:y:2009:i:02:p:467-488_09)
- Same paper, cost assumption: one-way transaction cost declining linearly from 10 bps per position switch on 1 Jan 1973 to 1.88 bps on 30 Jun 2005; interdealer spreads cited at "2 basis points or less" since the mid-1990s for $5-50m tickets. — [St. Louis Fed PDF](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2006/2006-046.pdf)
- Same paper, Sweeney (1986) filter rules, uniform portfolio across 10 currencies: original sample 1973:4-1980 net annual return 5.11%, average SR 0.84 (all currencies SR ≥ 0.68); ex-post sample 1981-2005:6 net return 2.92%, SR 0.41. — [St. Louis Fed PDF](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2006/2006-046.pdf)
- Same paper, Levich & Thomas (1993) filter + MA rules: in-sample 1976-1990 one-percent filter averaged 8.47% p.a. net across four currencies, MA(5,20) 9.09% p.a., average SR 0.63; ex-post 1991-2005:6 the one-percent filter averaged −1.14% p.a. (CHF −3.51%), uniform-portfolio mean net return −0.11%, average SR fell from 0.90 to −0.02. — [St. Louis Fed PDF](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2006/2006-046.pdf)
- Same paper, Taylor (1994) channel rules: mean net excess return fell from 5.74% p.a. (1982-1990) to 1.91% p.a. (1991-2005:6), none significantly > 0 in the later period. Genetic-programming rules (NWD 1997): 1981-Oct 1995 net 2.94% p.a., SR 0.36; Oct 1995-2005:6 net 0.15% p.a., SR 0.02, three of six rules negative. — [St. Louis Fed PDF](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2006/2006-046.pdf)
- Neely & Weller (2013), Journal of Banking & Finance 37(10): 3783-3798: trading-rule returns "persist in forex markets" but have "migrated to emerging markets to a considerable degree"; forex returns dipped in the 1990s, recovered by decade-end, superior to equities since 1998 (abstract only; no cost figures). — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:37:y:2013:i:10:p:3783-3798)
- Hsu, Taylor & Wang (2016), Journal of International Economics 102: 188-208: "daily data over 45 years", "30 developed and emerging market currencies", "over 21,000 technical rules", stepwise test for data snooping, "substantial predictability and excess profitability in both developed and emerging currencies", results "cross-validate[d] ... using out-of-sample analysis", attributed to "temporarily not-fully-rational behavior and market immaturity". — [WUSTL abstract](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/technical-trading-is-it-still-beating-the-foreign-exchange-market/); [CEPR DP10018](https://cepr.org/publications/DP10018)
- The 2013 working-paper version (Hsu & Taylor, "Forty Years, Thirty Currencies and 21,000 Trading Rules", data Jan 1971-Jul 2011): 21,195 rules (2,835 filter, 12,870 MA, 1,890 support-resistance, 3,000 channel breakout, 600 oscillator); fixed one-way cost 0.025%; developed-market predictability "strong in the 1970s and 1980s, then gradually disappeared from the early 1990s"; emerging-market predictability persisted into the 2000s; an equal-weighted portfolio of the 13 most predictable EM currencies each on its best rule gave 9% compound annual return, net SR 1.31 over ~24 years — but CXO notes this "include[s] compounded look-ahead bias, so they are 'ideal' rather than achievable". — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=26289)
- Schulmeister (2008), Applied Financial Economics 18(11): 917-930: 1,024 technical models on DEM(EUR)/USD; "each of the models would have been profitable over the entire sample period"; profitability "exclusively due to the exploitation of exchange rate trends"; 25 best in-sample performers mostly profitable OOS; "profitability of technical currency trading has declined since the late 1980s" (abstract only). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/apfiec/v18y2008i11p917-930.html)

**Intraday / high-frequency technical rules**
- Frömmel & Lampaert (2016), Finance Research Letters 18: 177-183, RUB/USD, "more than 8000 technical trading rules" on intraday data: "technical trading profits seem much more present on a higher frequency basis" but "the adjustment for real, rather than estimated transaction costs wipes away most of the profits"; rules at sufficiently high frequency "generate superior returns" only when "the central bank conducts a stabilizing exchange rate policy". — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v18y2016icp177-183.html); [Ghent record](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/7190535)
- Elaut, Frömmel & Lampaert (2018), Journal of Financial Markets 37: 35-51: RUB/USD on MICEX 2005-2014; intraday momentum defined as positive relation between first and last half-hour returns; driven by liquidity providers' "risk aversion to overnight holdings", not informed trading; stronger in crises (abstract only). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v37y2018icp35-51.html)
- Marshall, Cahan & Cahan (2008), Journal of Empirical Finance 15(2): 199-210 (US equities, intraday): "none of the 7846 popular technical trading rules we test are profitable after data snooping bias is taken into account". — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v15y2008i2p199-210.html)
- Schulmeister (2009), Review of Financial Economics 18(4): 190-201 (S&P 500 spot/futures): 2,580 momentum/reversal models; daily-data profitability "fell steadily from 1960 and has been negative since the early 1990s"; on 30-minute data models averaged 7.2% gross annual return 1983-2007 with no clear downward trend across eight subperiods, but "all 2,580 models performed worse" in 2001-2007 than in the 1980s/90s; author's two explanations: markets more efficient, or trends moved to frequencies higher than 30 minutes. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/revfin/v18y2009i4p190-201.html)

**Time-of-day and fixing effects (see KQ6 for strategy numbers)**
- Breedon & Ranaldo, "Intraday Patterns in FX Returns and Order Flow" (draft 5 Dec 2008; SNB WP 2011-04; published JMCB 45(5), 2013): EBS firm bid/ask data 1997-2007; "significant tendency for currencies to depreciate during local trading hours (e.g. EUR/USD tends to depreciate in the European morning and then appreciate in US trading hours)". Average EBS bid-ask spread 1997-2007: EUR/USD 0.017%, USD/JPY 0.018%, GBP/USD 0.056%, EUR/JPY 0.060%, USD/CHF 0.072%, AUD/USD 0.055%. — [AEA 2009 PDF](https://swlb1.aeaweb.org/conference/2009/retrieve.php?pdfid=301); [SNB WP 2011-04](https://www.snb.ch/en/publications/research/working-papers/2011/working_paper_2011_04); [JMCB record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:jmoncb:v:45:y:2013:i:5:p:953-965)
- Krohn, Mueller & Whelan, "Foreign Exchange Fixings and Returns Around the Clock" (Bank of Canada SWP 2021-48; Journal of Finance 79(1): 541-578, 2024): G9 currencies vs USD, 5-minute data Jan 1999-Dec 2019; dollar portfolio depreciates 5.0% p.a. before the Tokyo fix and appreciates 5.3% p.a. after; depreciates 4.2% p.a. until the ECB fix and appreciates ~4.6% p.a. from the London fix to NY close; t-stats 4.5-11.7; "roughly 2.5 basis point appreciation of the U.S. dollar before the respective fixes, followed by a depreciation of the same magnitude". — [Bank of Canada PDF](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf); [JoF record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:79:y:2024:i:1:p:541-578)
- Khademalomoom & Narayan (2019), JIFMIM 58: 65-77: hourly AUD, GBP, CAD, EUR, JPY, CHF vs USD, 2004-2014; "strong presence of time-of-the-day effects"; identifies a local-markets post-opening effect, a major-markets-activities effect and a markets-overlapping-times effect (abstract only; magnitudes not available). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfin/v58y2019icp65-77.html)

### Inferences
- The only academically documented intraday FX edges with published net-of-cost numbers are liquidity-provision/inventory patterns (time-of-day, fixing reversals), not indicator signals. Nothing in the peer-reviewed literature located here tests EMA crossovers, RSI/stochastic extremes, or Bollinger reversion on M1-M15 FX data and reports positive net expectancy.
- Every study that moves from estimated to real transaction costs (Frömmel & Lampaert; Krohn et al.) finds that most or all intraday gross profit is absorbed by the spread; the edge sizes (2.5 bp/day around fixes; 0.4-0.5 pip-equivalent) are of the same order as a raw-ECN round trip.
- Pre-2010 evidence (Neely et al., Breedon & Ranaldo) is old; Krohn et al. (data to 2019) partially confirm the European-hours dollar pattern but show the Tokyo-window strategy turned negative after ~2013 for EUR and GBP (see KQ6), i.e. decay is ongoing.

### Gaps
- No 2020-2026 peer-reviewed study of intraday indicator rules on EURUSD/XAUUSD after retail costs was found; the search budget ran out before Robot Wealth / Alpha Architect / recent SSRN queries could be run.
- Full text of Hsu-Taylor-Wang 2016 (HKU repository returned 403) — the developed-market Sharpe ratios in the published version could not be verified.
- Frömmel & Lampaert's actual gross/net return numbers and frequencies were not accessible (abstract only).

## KQ2. What do systematic backtests from quant sites / MQL5 / TradingView show for common scalping rules on M1-M15 charts, with and without costs?

### Takeaway
Across the only sizeable public library of standardized 10-year intraday backtests found (Backtrex, 25 strategies, Oct 2016-Oct 2026, 0.02% commission per side, no spread modeled), every indicator-based intraday FX rule (London breakout, MACD+RSI, Bollinger+stochastic, previous-day high/low breakout, ICT kill-zone FVG, VWAP fade) lost money with PF 0.64-0.87 and max drawdowns of 31-99%; the only intraday winners were long-only gold trend/mean-reversion rules on H1/H4 (PF 1.46-1.58, which still trailed buy-and-hold) and a 70-trade US30 M1 "liquidity sweep" test (PF 1.95) that its own author calls "not proof". Independent MT5 real-tick tests of a gold London breakout show PF 1.3-1.4 with 49% WR and 17-19% drawdown, but do not state spread/commission. A rigorously controlled scalper study (MQL5 forum, 2026) found zero directional edge across 31 features and 48 target/stop combos after costs.

### Inferences
- Backtrex's 0.02%-per-side commission equals ≈2.2 pips per side on EURUSD (~4.4 pips RT) and ≈2.6 pips per side on GBPUSD — heavier than a raw-ECN round trip (≈0.8-1.1 pips, KQ3) but with no spread or slippage at all. So the FX results are cost-pessimistic for an ECN scalper on commission yet cost-optimistic on execution; the sign of the result (PF 0.64-0.87) is unlikely to flip with realistic ECN costs because the gross edge before costs is itself near zero (Backtrex's own break-even analysis shows 33-36% WR against a 33.3% bracket requirement).
- The pattern that survives in the Backtrex library — low-frequency (130-251 trades/decade), long-only, wide-target rules on a secularly rising asset (gold) — is trend-beta harvesting, not scalping; all high-turnover intraday rules (1,000-9,000 trades/decade) lost, which matches the academic finding that turnover × cost dominates.
- The single credible positive scalping-timeframe result (gold London breakout, PF ~1.37 on ~715 trades) is (a) a session-volatility-breakout structure rather than an indicator signal, (b) unverified for spread/commission, and (c) partly in-sample (settings chosen on 2023-2025, which includes gold's strongest bull run in decades); its 157-trade 2026 OOS PF 1.61 is within noise of the in-sample figure.
- The MQL5 forum study is the only one found that applies a multiple-testing-corrected, random-entry-null protocol to a discretionary-style scalper; its conclusion (no feature beats random after costs; realized R:R degrades ~30% below design) is consistent with Marshall et al. and Frömmel-Lampaert.

### Gaps
- QuantifiedStrategies.com scalping/forex backtests are paywalled; no numeric results could be extracted. Quantpedia strategy #269 "Intraday Currency Seasonality" (2010-2014 backtest) page returned HTTP 503 twice.
- No Bollinger mean-reversion, RSI-extreme, or EMA-crossover test on M1/M5 FX with explicit spread+commission was found from a non-vendor source; MQL5 blog results are screenshot-only.
- Robot Wealth, Alpha Architect, QuantStart, Hudson & Thames, Au.Tra.Sy were not reached (search budget exhausted).
- Zarattini & Aziz "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?" (SSRN 4416622) returned 403; the ORB figures above are second-hand from the MQL5 thread.

## KQ3. How large are round-trip costs on EURUSD, GBPUSD, XAUUSD, US30, NAS100 at ECN/raw-spread MT4/MT5 brokers in 2025-2026, and what do they imply for required win rate/edge?

### Takeaway
Published raw-account spreads in 2025-26 are ~0.0-0.15 pip EURUSD, ~0.3-0.4 GBPUSD, ~$0.08-0.10/oz gold, ~1.0-1.4 points US30 and ~1.8 points USTEC, plus $3.00-3.50 per lot per side on FX/metals (indices commission-free); independently measured averages are 3-4x the published EURUSD figure (0.4 points) and worst-case spreads reach 177 points EURUSD and 586 points gold. A realistic EURUSD ECN round trip including slippage is ~1.5-2 pips, i.e. 15-40% of a 5-10 pip scalp target, pushing the break-even win rate for a 1:1 scalp to 60-70%.

## KQ4. What does published data on retail outcomes show (regulator loss rates, CFTC/NFA profitability, day-trader studies)? Is there data isolating scalpers?

### Takeaway
Every regulator-mandated dataset agrees: 65-75% of retail CFD/FX accounts lose money in any given quarter or 12-month window (ESMA 74-89% in 2018; broker risk-warnings 2026 averaging 71%; NFA/CFTC US forex 66-69% unprofitable per quarter through Q2 2026; CMC Canada 62-67% per quarter), and the academic studies that follow the same individuals over time find persistence of profit is far rarer than quarterly snapshots suggest (Brazil: 3% of 300+-day day traders net positive, 1.1% above minimum wage; Taiwan: <1% reliably profitable net of fees, 97% expected to lose). ### Inferences
- Quarterly snapshots (31-38% profitable) overstate long-run success because the population churns: the Brazilian panel shows profitability falling monotonically from 29.8% (one-day participants) to 3.0% (300+ days), and Barber et al. show that the losers supply 74% of the volume. For a scalper placing hundreds of trades per month, the relevant benchmark is the persistent-trader figure (≈1-3% net positive), not the quarterly 30-35%.
- The direction of the frequency effect is unambiguous in the two largest datasets (Taiwan, Brazil): more trading → worse net outcomes; Abbey-Doukas's opposite finding comes from a sample three orders of magnitude smaller.
- Retail FX loss rates (66-69% per quarter, OANDA US 2025-26) are slightly lower than EU CFD loss rates (71-89%), plausibly because US accounts are capped at 50:1 leverage and trade majors; the Heimer-Simsek natural experiment supports leverage as a causal driver of loss magnitude.

## KQ5. Does credible evidence support a "100% win rate" scalping method? What does the statistics of martingale/grid/averaging-down show about win rate vs. ruin?

### Takeaway
No credible evidence of a 100%-win-rate method exists in any source read; the only documented near-100% records are martingale/grid or no-stop EAs whose high win rates are purchased with catastrophic tails (an EA that won 94.13% of 801 deals needed 88.5% just to break even, with 45 trades carrying all profit; another with 74% WR went to zero). The academic treatment (Taranto & Khan 2020) confirms grid trading remains "subject to the risk of ruin"; a self-published 15-year AUD/CAD simulation puts classical martingale ruin at ~100% and even a heavily modified variant at 6-17% ruin.

### Cited Findings
- Taranto & Khan (2020), "Gambler's ruin problem and bi-directional grid constrained trading and investment strategies", Investment Management and Financial Innovations (issue 360): grid trading "whilst still subject to the risk of ruin, has the ability to generate significantly more profitable returns in the short term"; derives probability of ruin and expected steps to a barrier; grid takes longer to reach ruin than classic gambler's ruin (abstract via search; page fetch returned 405). — [Business Perspectives](https://www.businessperspectives.org/index.php/journals/investment-management-and-financial-innovations/issue-360/gambler-s-ruin-problem-and-bi-directional-grid-constrained-trading-and-investment-strategies)
- Taranto & Khan (2020), "Bi-directional grid absorption barrier constrained stochastic processes with applications in finance & investment", Risk Governance and Control 10(3): 20-33: models grid trading as a random walk between an absorption barrier at zero balance (ruin) and one at a profit target. — [Virtus Interpress](https://www.virtusinterpress.org/Bi-directional-grid-absorption-barrier-constrained-stochastic-processes-with.html)
- Self-published AUD/CAD M15 study 2010-2025 (no Monte Carlo method or simulation count documented; ranges stated without derivation — low reliability): classical doubling martingale (20-pip step) 15-year ruin "~100%", max DD 100%; + Fibonacci sizing: ruin 40-55%, DD 50-65%; + ATR filter and 48h time-stop: ruin 15-25%; + forced "grid reset" at −5 to −7% DD: ruin 5-15%, DD 5-7%, est. annual return 12-25%, skew-adjusted Sharpe 0.7-1.1, expected backtest-to-live degradation 30-50%; author concedes residual flash-crash/weekend-gap/broker risk and that the survival estimate "is imprecise because of superkurtosis". — [axiscoretech](https://axiscoretech.pages.dev/blog/trading-systems/martingale-absolute-audcad)
- MQL5 forum (Sep 2026) reported client EAs: one "needed 88.50% to break even and won 94.13%; 45 of its 801 deals accounted for all its earnings" (over 60 trades that margin is ~3 trades vs ~4-trade noise SD); another "averaging 4.80 per win against 19.14 per loss, needed about 80% and won 74%; the test account went to zero". — [MQL5 forum 517101](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/517101)
- Backtrex: VWAP fade with 59.17% WR lost 98.6% because target (30 pts) < stop (50 pts) + costs; break-even WR 62.5% before costs; "A profit factor above 2 on M1 data is treated as a warning sign." — [Backtrex VWAP](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/vwap-band-fade-dax); [Backtrex guide](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/intraday-scalping-strategy-backtesting)
- Chague et al.: the single best of 1,551 persistent Brazilian day traders averaged US$310/day with a daily SD of US$2,560 (≈8x the mean); the eight above-bank-teller earners had daily SDs of US$632-3,308. — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/)
- Barber et al.: even the top 500 of ~360,000 Taiwanese day traders earned 28.1-37.9 bps/day net, i.e. far from "always winning", and <1% of the population is reliably profitable. — [EconPapers JFM](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24)

### Inferences (derived — standard gambler's-ruin arithmetic, not from a source)
- A doubling martingale with per-trade loss probability q and capital for N doublings (exposure 2^(N+1) − 1 units) has per-cycle ruin probability q^(N+1) and per-cycle "win rate" 1 − q^(N+1). With q = 0.5, N = 7 (255-unit max exposure): per-cycle WR 99.6%, per-cycle ruin 0.39%; survival over 500 cycles = (1 − 1/256)^500 ≈ 14%, over 1,000 cycles ≈ 2%. Expected value per cycle before costs = (255/256)(+1) − (1/256)(255) = 0; after spread/commission/swap it is negative. The reported win rate therefore carries no information about expectancy.
- The MQL5-thread EA statistics (94% WR, 88.5% break-even, 45/801 deals = all profit) are the empirical signature of exactly this payoff structure: expectancy is decided by a handful of tail trades, and the sample needed to distinguish a true 94% from the 88.5% break-even is far larger than the 60-800 trades such EAs are typically judged on.
- Grid/averaging systems cannot be validated by MT4/MT5 tester drawdown on a few years of data because the ruin event is rare by construction; Taranto & Khan's barrier framework implies ruin probability → 1 as the horizon grows for any finite capital unless the strategy has genuine positive drift net of costs.

### Gaps
- Full text of Taranto & Khan (formulas, numeric ruin tables) not retrieved (405 / 403 on three URL variants).
- No peer-reviewed study of retail grid/martingale EA outcomes (e.g., from Myfxbook or MQL5 Signals data) was found.
- No source documents a verified live track record with a sustained ≥95% WR and positive expectancy; absence of evidence is noted, not proof of impossibility.

## KQ6. Which strategy families retain the most robust evidence of edge at short horizons in 2024-2026, and what caveats apply?

### Takeaway
The best-documented short-horizon edges are structural liquidity patterns — the post-London-fix (11:00-17:00 ET) dollar depreciation and pre-ECB-fix dollar appreciation (EUR: 8.64% p.a., SR 1.03 at half the quoted spread, 1999-2019; 1.52% p.a., SR 0.05 at full spread) and the older Breedon-Ranaldo EUR/USD "short 07:00-13:00 / long 13:00-21:00 GMT" rule (6-7% p.a., SR 1.3/0.9 net of interdealer spreads, 1997-2007) — followed by session-open volatility breakouts on gold (PF ~1.37, 2023-26, costs unstated). Indicator-based scalps (MACD/RSI/Bollinger/EMA cross on M1-H1 FX) have no positive evidence. Caveats: all time-of-day effects are regime-dependent (Tokyo-fix strategy negative after 2013), cost-fragile (sign flips between 50% and 100% of quoted spread), and the authors themselves say "it is not obvious that this can be exploited by the average trader".

- Backtrex: "Each additional free parameter needs roughly 200 more trades"; 30% OOS holdout; <100 trades insufficient, 500 recommended, 1,000 to segment by session; 200 trades at PF 1.4 implies a live range of ~1.1-1.7. — [Backtrex guide](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/intraday-scalping-strategy-backtesting)

### Inferences
- Ranked by evidence quality (sample length, cost treatment, independence): (1) post-London-fix / pre-ECB-fix EUR and GBP drift — 21 years, peer-reviewed (JoF 2024), positive at half-spread, marginal at full spread, holding periods of 3-6 hours (not scalping); (2) EUR/USD local-hours rule — 11 years, net of firm interdealer spreads, SR 0.9-1.3, but data end 2007 and Krohn's half-spread results suggest the morning leg weakened; (3) gold session-open breakout — 3.7 years, two brokers, costs unstated, partly in-sample; (4) everything indicator-based on M1-H1 FX — negative or absent.
- Current retail raw spreads on EURUSD (0.1-0.4 pip + $7 RT) are at or below the 1997-2007 EBS spreads Breedon-Ranaldo used (≈2 pips) and around Krohn's "50% of indicative BA" case, so cost is no longer the binding constraint for a retail trader on EURUSD; decay of the pattern itself (post-2013 Tokyo-window reversal) is.
- The fact that reversal returns load on VIX implies these are compensation for intermediation risk that spikes when retail execution is worst (wide spreads, slippage); the realized edge for an MT4/MT5 trader is therefore likely lower than Table IX's half-spread case.

### Gaps
- No quantified backtest of "Asian-session mean reversion" or "news-event (NFP/CPI) scalping" with costs was found before the search budget ran out; evidence absent, not negative.
- No post-2019 update of the fixing-reversal strategy; whether the European-window edge persisted through 2020-2026 is unknown.

## KQ7. What are realistic performance benchmarks for a profitable intraday systematic FX strategy (annual return, Sharpe, max DD)?

### Takeaway
Reputable net-of-cost figures for intraday FX cluster at single-digit annual returns and Sharpe 0.1-1.3 (Krohn et al. 0.12-1.03 at half-spread; Breedon-Ranaldo 0.9-1.3; Neely et al. historical daily rules 0.4-0.9 falling to ~0 after 1990); practitioner validation guidance treats PF 1.3-1.7 as the realistic target band, PF > 2 on M1 as a red flag, and expects 30-50% degradation from backtest to live. The one >1 Sharpe academic result for daily FX rules (EM portfolio, SR 1.31) embeds look-ahead bias.

### Cited Findings
- Krohn et al. (1999-2019): Sharpe "ranging between 0.12 and 1.03" for traders able to trade at 50% of indicative spreads; best cell EUR Europe-window 8.64% p.a., SR 1.03; annualized SDs "generally below 8% for all currencies". — [Bank of Canada PDF](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf)
- Breedon & Ranaldo (1997-2007): EUR/USD time-of-day legs 6-7% p.a., SR 1.3 and 0.9, net of EBS spreads. — [AEA PDF](https://swlb1.aeaweb.org/conference/2009/retrieve.php?pdfid=301)
- Neely, Weller & Ulrich: daily filter/MA/channel rules net 5-9% p.a. with SR 0.63-0.90 in-sample (1973-1990); 0.15-2.92% p.a. with SR −0.02 to 0.41 ex post (1991-2005). — [St. Louis Fed PDF](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2006/2006-046.pdf)
- Hsu & Taylor (1971-2011): best-rule EM portfolio 9% CAR, net SR 1.31, "no significant drawdowns" — with compounded look-ahead bias ("ideal rather than achievable"). — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=26289)
- Backtrex 10-year intraday winners: gold EMA 8/21 H1 CAGR 8.1%, max DD −16.7%, PF 1.46; gold stochastic H4 CAGR 7.4%, DD −11.4%, PF 1.58, Sharpe 0.70 — both below buy-and-hold gold (CAGR 12.3%). — [Backtrex EMA gold](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/ema-8-21-crossover-gold); [Backtrex Stoch gold](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/stochastic-oversold-gold)
- Backtrex validation benchmarks for scalping systems: PF 1.3-1.7; WR 55-70%; avg win/avg loss 0.8-1.2; PF > 2 on M1 "a warning sign"; backtests without tick data and slippage "overstate real performance by 30 to 50%"; slippage alone cuts PF 15-25%; vendor claims <2% divergence from live MetaTrader execution; M1 scalping yields 500-1,000 trades/yr; prop-firm limits typically 5% daily / 10% total DD. — [Backtrex guide](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/intraday-scalping-strategy-backtesting)
- MQL5 gold breakout: 15-19% max equity DD at 1% risk "normal for this system"; author recommends 0.3-0.4% risk to fit a 10% prop-firm limit; PF 1.32-1.38. — [MQL5 blog 777540](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777540)
- Prop-firm realized outcomes as an external benchmark: 7% of 300,000+ accounts ever paid out; average payout ≈4% of funded size; pass rates 5-18%. — [Finance Magnates 2026](https://financemagnates.com/forex/prop-firm-e8-markets-warns-retail-traders-off-cfd-brokers-as-industry-leans-harder-on-educational-labels/)
- Top-decile retail outcomes: Taiwan top-500 day traders 28.1-37.9 bps/day net (≈ +5 pp annual alpha on total portfolio per 2011 WP); Brazil's best persistent day trader US$310/day with SD US$2,560. — [Berkeley WP PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/)
- Self-published martingale-variant claim of 12-25% p.a. with Sharpe 0.7-1.1 and 6-17% 15-year ruin — undocumented methodology, not a benchmark. — [axiscoretech](https://axiscoretech.pages.dev/blog/trading-systems/martingale-absolute-audcad)

### Inferences
- A defensible benchmark for an honestly costed intraday FX/CFD system from these sources: net CAGR in the mid-single digits to low teens at ~1% risk per trade, Sharpe roughly 0.5-1.0, PF 1.3-1.6, max DD 10-20%, with ≥500 trades and a true OOS period before any of those numbers are trusted. Claims materially above this (PF > 2 on M1, Sharpe > 2, DD < 5% with high turnover) have, in every case located here, come with look-ahead bias, unstated costs, martingale tails or vendor incentive.
- A 30-50% haircut to backtest return and a 15-25% haircut to PF for slippage (Backtrex) applied to the gold-breakout PF 1.37 gives ~1.05-1.15 — i.e. the best independently reported scalping-timeframe system is near break-even once execution is included.

### Gaps
- No reputable source gives a distribution of live Sharpe ratios for retail intraday FX systems (e.g., from Myfxbook or MQL5 Signals); the benchmarks above are inferred from a handful of academic and site backtests.
- No 2024-2026 academic paper reports net Sharpe for an FX intraday strategy; the newest cost-adjusted academic Sharpe figures are Krohn et al.'s (data through 2019).
